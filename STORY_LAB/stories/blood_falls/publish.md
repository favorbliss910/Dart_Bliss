# Blood Falls, Antarctica: publish kit

Facts:
- Blood Falls is at the snout of Taylor Glacier, in the McMurdo Dry Valleys.
- It was discovered in 1911 by geologist Thomas Griffith Taylor; red algae were blamed at first.
- The cause is ancient, salty, iron-rich brine trapped under the ice that rusts on contact with air.
- A 2023 Johns Hopkins study (Livi et al., Frontiers in Astronomy and Space Sciences) found iron-rich nanospheres about 100x smaller than a red blood cell.
- Those nanospheres are not crystalline minerals, so rover instruments could miss them on Mars.
- Microbes live in the brine with no light or oxygen (Mikucki et al., Science, 2009). That makes it a model for life on Mars and on icy moons like Europa.
