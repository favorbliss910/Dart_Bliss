# The Carrington Event (1859): publish kit

Facts check out:
- Carrington saw the flare on 1 Sept 1859 from Redhill, south of London; Richard Hodgson saw it too.
- The storm hit about 17.5 hours later.
- Boston and Portland operators worked on "auroral current" alone for about 2 hours.
- Aurora was seen as far south as Cuba and the Caribbean.
- Rocky Mountain miners got up to make breakfast.
- July 2012 near miss: NASA said Earth would have been hit "if the eruption had occurred only one week earlier".
- Probability: NASA's article quotes Pete Riley's (2012) estimate of about 12% per decade; later studies (e.g. Love) give lower numbers. A range of "about 1-12%" covers the published estimates.

## Description
In 1859, a telegraph operator in Boston unplugged his battery... and kept sending messages for two hours. ⚡ The power was coming from the sky.

The day before, astronomer Richard Carrington watched two white flashes burst out of the Sun, the first solar flare anyone had ever seen. About 17 hours later it slammed into Earth. Telegraph wires threw sparks, paper caught fire, and the northern lights showed up as far south as Cuba. Gold miners in the Rockies woke in the middle of the night and started cooking breakfast, sure it was morning.

The scary part? In July 2012, a storm just as strong ripped through Earth's orbit. We missed it by about a week.

⚠️ The miners story comes from an 1859 Rocky Mountain News report. Experts disagree on the odds of another hit; published estimates range from a few percent up to about 12% per decade.

Sources: NASA Science ("Near Miss: The Solar Superstorm of July 2012"), NOAA, National Research Council (2008), USGS / Love et al., Wikipedia "Carrington Event"
