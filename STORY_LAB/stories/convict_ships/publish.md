## Description
One in four people died on these ships. Then the government changed one line in a contract, and almost all of them lived.

In 1790, Britain shipped convicts to Australia, and captains were paid for every prisoner who came aboard. Feeding them less meant more profit. On the Second Fleet, roughly a quarter never made it. Later contracts tied pay to convicts landed alive, with bonuses for ships' surgeons, and death rates fell to a few percent.

Same ships. Same captains. Same ocean. The only thing that changed was what they got paid for.

What does *your* job pay for? 👇
