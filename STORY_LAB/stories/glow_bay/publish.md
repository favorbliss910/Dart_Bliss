Facts to keep straight:
- Mosquito Bay is on the island of Vieques, Puerto Rico.
- In 2008 Guinness World Records named it the brightest bioluminescent bay.
- The glow comes from the dinoflagellate Pyrodinium bahamense, up to about 700,000 per gallon.
- Hurricane Maria (2017) dimmed it, and the glow recovered within months.
- Swimming has been banned there since 2007; visitors see it from kayaks and clear-bottom kayaks. So the cover shows a hand in the water from a kayak, not a swimmer.

## Description
Touch the water in this bay at night and it explodes into electric blue. 💙

This is Mosquito Bay on Vieques, Puerto Rico. Guinness World Records named it the brightest bioluminescent bay on Earth. The light comes from tiny single-celled dinoflagellates, up to 700,000 in a gallon of water, that flash blue whenever the water moves. Every paddle stroke and every trailing hand leaves a glowing trail.

In 2017, Hurricane Maria plunged the bay into darkness. Within months, the glow came back.

Visiting? Swimming isn't allowed, to protect the dinoflagellates, so tours go out by kayak on dark, moonless nights.
