# Lake Nyos (21 Aug 1986): publish kit

Facts:
- About 1,700 people died (often given as 1,746), plus about 3,500 livestock.
- The CO2 cloud flowed up to about 25 km down the valleys.
- The lake turned reddish-brown as iron-rich deep water rose and oxidised.
- Degassing pipes were installed from 2001 (more in 2011). They greatly reduce the risk, but scientists don't say it can "never" happen again.

## Description
On the night of August 21, 1986, Lake Nyos in Cameroon did something no one thought a lake could do. It exploded, silently.

A massive cloud of carbon dioxide burst from the bottom of the lake, rolled over the crater rim and flowed down into the valleys below. Invisible. Odourless. Heavier than air. By morning, about 1,700 people and thousands of animals in nearby villages had suffocated in their sleep. The lake's blue water had turned blood-red.

Scientists call it a "limnic eruption". Today, pipes in the lake slowly release the built-up gas, to stop it from ever building up like that again.
