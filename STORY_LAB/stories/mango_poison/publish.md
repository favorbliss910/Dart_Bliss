# The Mango Poison: publish kit

Facts:
- Mango, cashew and poison ivy are all in the same plant family (Anacardiaceae).
- Mango peel and sap contain urushiol-like compounds that can cause "mango dermatitis" around the mouth.
- The cashew shell holds a caustic liquid (anacardic acid and cardol, relatives of urushiol). That's why cashews are sold shelled and heat-treated.
- 1982: in Pennsylvania, cashews contaminated with shell fragments, sold for a Little League fundraiser, gave 54 people a rash. Reported in the medical literature as a dermatitis outbreak. Double-check the citation before quoting exact details.

## Description
Mangoes are related to poison ivy. 🥭

Mango peel carries chemicals very close to urushiol, the oil behind the poison ivy rash. That's why some people get an itchy rash around their lips after biting into an unpeeled mango.

And it's the same family that gave us cashews. That's why you never see cashews sold in their shells: the shell is full of a caustic oil that burns skin.

In 1982, cashews sold for a Little League fundraiser had bits of shell mixed in, and 54 people broke out in a rash.

So next time you bite into a mango... remember who its cousins are. 🌿
