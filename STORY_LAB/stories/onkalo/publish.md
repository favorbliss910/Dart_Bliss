# Onkalo: Finland's 100,000-Year Warning: publish kit

Facts:
- Onkalo is on Olkiluoto island in Eurajoki, Finland.
- Spent fuel goes about 400-450 m down, in copper canisters packed in bentonite clay.
- Operations are expected to start in the mid-2020s; the site is due to be sealed in the 2120s and must isolate the waste for about 100,000 years.
- The line "This place is not a place of honor..." comes from a 1993 Sandia National Laboratories report for the US WIPP site in New Mexico, not from Onkalo itself. Say "scientists once wrote..." rather than presenting it as Onkalo's sign.

## Description
"This place is not a place of honor. No highly esteemed deed is commemorated here. Nothing valued is here." ☢️

Scientists once wrote that message for a nuclear waste site meant to stay dangerous for thousands of years. But here's the problem: how do you warn someone 100,000 years in the future?

Languages die. Writing systems vanish. English might mean nothing to them. And a giant scary monument might not scare them at all; it might look like treasure.

Finland's answer is Onkalo: tunnels carved about 400 metres down into solid granite, where spent nuclear fuel will be sealed in copper canisters. The plan is to close it in the 2120s and leave it untouched for 100,000 years, far longer than all of recorded human history.

The hardest part isn't the engineering. It's making sure nobody ever goes looking.
