## Description
Why would a king put armed guards around a field of… potatoes? 🥔👑

Legend says a European kingdom was going hungry, and the king had the perfect food: potatoes. Cheap, filling, easy to grow. But nobody wanted them. So instead of forcing people, he tricked them.

He planted potatoes beside his palace and surrounded them with guards, as if they were royal treasure. The villagers figured: if the king guards them like gold, they must be worth stealing. So they crept in at night and took them, while the guards quietly looked the other way.

Soon potatoes were growing in gardens all over the kingdom.

That's reverse psychology: tell people they can't have something, and suddenly they want it.

The story is usually told about Antoine Parmentier in France or Frederick the Great of Prussia. Historians say the guarded-field part is probably legend, but the trick still works today.
