## Part 1 — Psychoacoustics, Spectrum, Dynamics, Loudness & Mixing (1–200)

1. **Mix for the ear's sweet spot** — Treat 2–5 kHz as the most sensitive band; place narration consonant energy there and keep competing elements 3–6 dB lower in that range.
2. **Respect equal-loudness curves** — Judge low-end balance at a calibrated ~75–79 dB SPL, because at quieter phone-listening levels bass below 100 Hz drops away perceptually.
3. **Check at whisper volume** — Audition the full mix at roughly 50 dB SPL; anything that vanishes there will vanish for a viewer scrolling quietly in bed.
4. **Masking spreads upward** — Remember low sounds mask higher ones far more than the reverse, so a 200 Hz pad cut matters more for speech than a 6 kHz cut.
5. **Critical bands decide collisions** — Two sounds within the same critical band (~1/3 octave above 500 Hz) fight; separate them by at least one-third octave or by time.
6. **Temporal masking window** — A loud hit masks quieter sounds for ~50–200 ms after it, so never place a key narration word within 100 ms after a big impact.
7. **Pre-masking is short** — Loud transients can hide sounds up to ~5–20 ms before them; avoid stacking a soft click right ahead of a cartoon whack.
8. **Precedence effect sets location** — The first arrival within ~1–30 ms defines perceived direction, so keep narration's direct signal earliest and any doubles delayed beyond 30 ms or not at all.
9. **Avoid comb filtering in doubles** — Delays of 1–15 ms between correlated copies cause comb filtering; offset layered calls by over 20 ms or decorrelate them.
10. **Transient perception needs ~10 ms** — The ear reads attack character in the first 5–15 ms, so preserve that window on stingers, mimic calls and comedic hits.
11. **Duration affects loudness** — Sounds shorter than ~200 ms seem quieter than their peak suggests; boost brief comedic blips 2–4 dB relative to sustained elements.
12. **The missing fundamental** — Phone speakers cannot reproduce below ~300 Hz, so add harmonics at 2–4× the fundamental to imply bass the ear will reconstruct.
13. **Harshness lives at 2.5–4 kHz** — Ear-canal resonance makes 2.5–4 kHz fatiguing; boost there sparingly and never more than 3 dB on non-dialogue layers.
14. **Sibilance zone** — Expect "s" energy at 5–9 kHz; control it in narration before it hits a limiter that will exaggerate it on earbuds.
15. **Fatigue is cumulative** — Over three minutes, constant high-mid density tires listeners; give the 2–5 kHz band short rest moments every 20–30 seconds.
16. **Loudness is relative** — Contrast creates impact, so drop the bed 6–10 dB for 0.5–1 s before a big moment rather than pushing the moment louder.
17. **Spectral novelty grabs attention** — The ear notices new frequency content faster than level changes; announce the drongo's mimicry with a band nothing else occupies.
18. **Informational masking is real** — Competing intelligible speech or lyrics masks narration far more than noise; never play vocal-led music or chatter under narration.
19. **Interaural cues collapse on phones** — Most phones play near-mono; don't rely on stereo separation to unmask narration from music.
20. **Binaural unmasking on earbuds** — On earbuds, centring narration while widening music gains ~3 dB effective clarity; use it as a bonus, never as the only fix.
21. **Pitch salience aids separation** — A steady harmonic voice separates from noisy beds; keep ambience and textures noise-like and narration clearly harmonic.
22. **Onset synchrony fuses sounds** — Elements starting within ~20 ms fuse into one event; offset them deliberately if they must read as separate.
23. **Auditory streaming by pitch** — Rapid alternating sounds more than ~4 semitones apart split into separate streams; use this to keep meerkat chatter distinct from narration.
24. **The ear compresses loudness** — A 10 dB increase sounds roughly twice as loud; make deliberate level contrasts at least 6 dB to register on small speakers.
25. **JND for level is ~1 dB** — Don't chase sub-0.5 dB moves on individual tracks; make mix decisions in steps of at least 1 dB.
26. **Short-term memory for timbre fades** — Compare A/B switches within 2–3 seconds and level-match within 0.5 dB, or louder will always win.
27. **Reverb reduces intelligibility** — Late energy over ~50 ms after speech smears consonants; keep narration's reverb-to-direct ratio below −15 dB or bypass it.
28. **Clarity index target** — Aim for C50 above +10 dB on narration, meaning early energy dominates late reflections and words stay crisp.
29. **Upward masking of air** — Strong 1–2 kHz content hides air above 8 kHz; don't add sparkle EQ until the mids beneath are controlled.
30. **Ear adapts to steady tones** — A constant hum fades from perception within seconds but still masks; filter steady drones even if you've stopped hearing them.
31. **Narration owns 1–4 kHz** — Reserve 1–4 kHz for the narrator and cut music and ambience there by 2–5 dB whenever the narrator speaks.
32. **Find the voice's fundamental** — Identify the narrator's fundamental (typically 85–180 Hz male, 165–255 Hz female) and high-pass just below it.
33. **High-pass narration** — Apply a 12–18 dB/oct high-pass at 70–100 Hz on narration to remove rumble, plosive energy and handling noise.
34. **Tame narration mud** — Cut 2–4 dB at 200–400 Hz on narration with a medium Q (~1.0–1.5) when it sounds boxy on phone speakers.
35. **Presence lift for narration** — Add a gentle 1–3 dB shelf or bell around 3–5 kHz to narration for intelligibility, checked against sibilance.
36. **Nasal zone check** — If the narrator sounds honky, cut 2–3 dB around 800 Hz–1.2 kHz with a narrow Q of 2–4.
37. **Chest warmth only if audible** — Boost 120–200 Hz on narration only for earbud warmth, and cap it at +2 dB since phone speakers ignore it.
38. **Air sparingly** — A 1–2 dB high shelf at 10–12 kHz adds polish on earbuds, but stop if it raises noise floor or sibilance.
39. **Complementary EQ** — Wherever you boost narration, cut the same frequency on the music bus by a similar amount (2–4 dB) for mirrored clarity.
40. **Dynamic EQ over static cuts** — Use dynamic EQ on music at 1–4 kHz keyed from narration, dipping 3–6 dB only while words are present.
41. **Multiband sidechain beats broadband ducking** — Duck only the 300 Hz–5 kHz band of music under narration so the beat and air survive.
42. **High-pass everything not bass** — Put high-pass filters on every non-bass element, typically 100–200 Hz for ambience and SFX layers, to clear the low end.
43. **Phone low-end floor** — Treat ~250–300 Hz as the effective low limit of phone speakers and ensure every important sound has energy above it.
44. **Earbud low-end reality** — Earbuds reproduce 40–60 Hz well, so control sub energy; don't let unchecked sub-bass become boomy in sealed buds.
45. **Low-pass sub content at 30 Hz** — Remove everything below 25–30 Hz on the master; it eats headroom and no listening device reproduces it usefully.
46. **One owner per low band** — Assign 40–80 Hz to one element at a time (music bass or impact), never both simultaneously.
47. **Saturate for small-speaker bass** — Use harmonic exciters or soft saturation on bass and impacts to generate 200–600 Hz harmonics phones can play.
48. **Mono-ize below 150 Hz** — Collapse stereo content below 120–150 Hz to mono to avoid phase cancellation and wasted energy on phones.
49. **Cut before boosting** — Reach for 2–4 dB subtractive cuts first; boosts above 4 dB usually signal a source or arrangement problem.
50. **Narrow cuts, wide boosts** — Use Q 4–10 for surgical cuts on resonances and Q 0.5–1.0 for tonal boosts.
51. **Sweep for resonances carefully** — When boost-and-sweep hunting resonances, cut only those clearly ringing and limit to 3–4 notches per track.
52. **EQ in context** — Make final EQ decisions with narration playing; soloed EQ choices rarely survive the full mix.
53. **Reduce 2–4 kHz in music beds** — Music mixes are often bright; a 2–3 dB wide cut at 2–4 kHz on the score bed makes narration sit without lowering fader.
54. **Tame cymbals and hiss above 8 kHz** — Shelf down 8–12 kHz by 1–3 dB on busy beds so narration sibilance doesn't compete with sizzle.
55. **Watch 500 Hz build-up** — Summed tracks accumulate around 300–600 Hz; check the mix bus there and trim 1–2 dB if the whole mix feels cloudy.
56. **Frequency slotting for SFX** — Give recurring sound categories a home band, e.g. footsteps 1–3 kHz, impacts 60–200 Hz plus 2 kHz click, so they don't overlap.
57. **Mimic calls need their own slot** — Place the drongo's alarm-call mimicry in 2–6 kHz bursts timed between narration phrases, never under words.
58. **Use spectrum analyzers as confirmation** — Verify with an analyzer (1/6-octave smoothing) only after listening; trust ears first, graphs second.
59. **Linear phase for master tonal moves** — Use linear-phase EQ for broad master shelves to avoid smearing transients, but minimum phase on individual tracks.
60. **Mind pre-ringing** — Avoid steep linear-phase filters on percussive content; their pre-echo blurs cartoon hits.
61. **Filter slopes matter** — Use 12 dB/oct high-passes for natural results and 24–48 dB/oct only for problem rumble below 60 Hz.
62. **Proximity effect correction** — If narration was recorded close, apply a low shelf cut of 2–4 dB below 200 Hz to undo proximity boom.
63. **De-ess before compression** — Place a de-esser at 5–8 kHz ahead of heavy compression so the compressor doesn't react to sibilant peaks.
64. **De-ess gently** — Aim for 3–6 dB of de-essing reduction; more causes lisping that phone speakers make obvious.
65. **Plosive control** — Remove plosives with a high-pass or a dynamic band at 60–150 Hz, triggered only when the pop exceeds the phrase by 6 dB.
66. **Mouth noise removal** — Clean clicks and lip smacks on narration before compression, since 4:1 compression raises them by roughly 6–10 dB.
67. **Match narration tone across sessions** — Match EQ curves between pickup lines and main narration within 1 dB per octave band so cuts are invisible.
68. **Reference curve for narration** — Compare narration's long-term spectrum to a trusted broadcast VO; deviations above 3 dB in any octave deserve a look.
69. **Avoid over-brightening for phones** — Phone speakers already peak around 2–4 kHz; don't pre-boost that range above +3 dB or the mix becomes shrill.
70. **Give character voices their own EQ** — Treat any meerkat character lines with a distinct EQ curve (e.g., +2 dB at 2.5 kHz, high-pass 150 Hz) so they never mask narration tonally.
71. **Spectral balance target** — Keep the master's long-term spectrum roughly following a pink-noise tilt of −3 dB/octave, falling slightly faster above 10 kHz.
72. **Tilt check** — If the mix feels thin on phones, tilt the master 1 dB toward 200–800 Hz rather than adding sub.
73. **Notch hum precisely** — Remove 50/60 Hz hum and its harmonics (100/120, 150/180 Hz) with notches of Q 10+ rather than broad cuts.
74. **Clean noise before EQ boosts** — Run noise reduction on narration before EQ, since presence boosts raise hiss by the same amount.
75. **Limit noise reduction depth** — Keep spectral noise reduction to 6–12 dB of reduction to avoid watery artifacts that earbuds expose.
76. **Mid/side EQ for music beds** — Cut 1–4 kHz by 2–4 dB on the mid channel of music only, keeping the sides bright and the centre free for narration.
77. **Watch stereo phase on music** — Keep music bed correlation above +0.3 so mono fold-down on phones doesn't hollow it out.
78. **Remove sub from reverb returns** — High-pass reverb returns at 200–300 Hz and low-pass at 6–8 kHz to keep tails out of narration's space.
79. **Dark reverbs for clarity** — Use reverbs with high-frequency damping so tails don't sit in the 3–6 kHz consonant band.
80. **EQ before saturation for control** — High-pass before saturation stages so low-frequency content doesn't generate muddy intermodulation.
81. **Compress narration in stages** — Use two compressors at 2–3 dB gain reduction each rather than one doing 6 dB, for transparent density.
82. **Narration compression starting point** — Start at 3:1 to 4:1 ratio, 5–15 ms attack, 50–100 ms release, aiming for 3–6 dB of gain reduction.
83. **Peak-catch the narration** — Follow the main compressor with a fast limiter or 10:1 compressor catching only the top 2–3 dB of peaks.
84. **Narration dynamic range target** — Keep narration's short-term loudness within a ~6 dB window so no word drops out on phones.
85. **Ride gain before compressing** — Clip-gain or automate narration to even out phrase levels within ±2 dB before any compressor touches it.
86. **Use a vocal rider for consistency** — A vocal-rider plugin with ±6 dB range gives smoother results than heavy compression on long narration.
87. **Optical-style for smoothness** — Use program-dependent (opto-style) compression on narration for natural, even release on varied phrases.
88. **Fast attack kills consonants** — Attacks under 2 ms flatten consonant punch; keep narration attack at 5 ms or slower unless taming spikes.
89. **Release to the speech rhythm** — Set release so gain recovers between syllables (60–120 ms); too long causes pumping, too short causes distortion.
90. **Watch the knee** — Use a soft knee (6–10 dB) for narration and a hard knee for SFX peak control.
91. **Makeup gain honestly** — Match compressed and bypassed levels within 0.5 dB when judging compression, or loudness bias will fool you.
92. **Parallel compression for SFX weight** — Blend a heavily compressed (10:1, −15 dB GR) copy of the SFX bus at −10 to −15 dB to thicken without losing transients.
93. **Parallel compression on narration** — Blend a heavily squashed copy of narration at −12 dB under the dry voice for density without audible pumping.
94. **Preserve transients on hits** — Use 20–30 ms attack on SFX bus compression so the initial 10–20 ms of cartoon impacts passes through.
95. **Transient shaping before limiting** — Boost attacks 2–4 dB with a transient designer on small hits so they still read after the master limiter.
96. **Soften harsh transients** — Reduce attack by 2–6 dB on clicky sounds that spike peaks without adding perceived loudness.
97. **Clip instead of limit for fast peaks** — Soft-clip single-sample peaks of 1–3 dB on drum-like SFX; it's more transparent than a limiter for very short spikes.
98. **Don't over-limit transients** — If a limiter is reducing more than 3 dB on a single hit, lower the hit's fader instead.
99. **Sidechain-duck music under narration** — Duck the music bus 4–8 dB when narration plays, using a sidechain compressor or ducker keyed from the narration bus.
100. **Ducking attack and release** — Use 10–50 ms attack and 300–800 ms release on narration ducking so music swells naturally between sentences, not between syllables.
101. **Hold the duck through short gaps** — Add 150–300 ms of hold so music doesn't pop up in brief pauses between words.
102. **Lookahead ducking** — Use 5–20 ms lookahead or a pre-delayed key so music dips before the first syllable instead of after it.
103. **Duck ambience less than music** — Duck ambience 2–4 dB under narration versus 4–8 dB for music, keeping the Kalahari environment present.
104. **Duck SFX selectively** — Duck only sustained SFX under narration; leave punctuating hits unducked and schedule them between phrases.
105. **Use a narration key bus** — Feed the sidechain from a dedicated pre-fader narration send so fader rides don't change ducking depth.
106. **Filter the sidechain key** — High-pass the sidechain key at 150–200 Hz so plosives and breaths don't trigger ducking.
107. **Exclude breaths from ducking** — Gate or lower breaths 6–10 dB in the key signal so music doesn't dip on inhales.
108. **Automate where ducking fails** — Replace automatic ducking with hand-drawn automation on critical lines; set duck depth by ear phrase by phrase.
109. **Gate gently** — Use downward expansion (1:2, −6 to −10 dB range) on narration rather than hard gates, which chop word tails.
110. **Expander threshold below speech** — Set narration expander threshold 6–10 dB below the quietest word so soft syllables stay intact.
111. **Bus compression as glue** — Apply 1–2 dB gain reduction at 2:1 with slow attack (30 ms) and auto release on the mix bus for cohesion.
112. **Avoid stacked compression pumping** — Ensure total gain reduction across track, bus and master on narration doesn't exceed ~10 dB.
113. **Upward compression for whispers** — Use upward compression (+3 to +6 dB) to lift quiet narration asides without crushing loud lines.
114. **Multiband compression on narration** — Use a low band (below 250 Hz) compressing 2–3 dB to stop boominess on emphatic words without affecting clarity.
115. **Sustain control on SFX tails** — Shorten SFX sustain by 3–6 dB with transient shaping so tails don't blanket the next narration word.
116. **Compression ratio for music beds** — Keep music bed compression mild (1.5:1–2:1) since score dynamics provide the emotional arc.
117. **Avoid dynamic flatness** — Keep at least 6–8 LU of variation in short-term loudness across the piece so comedic beats have room to land.
118. **Silence is a dynamic tool** — Drop everything but narration, or to full silence for 200–500 ms, before a punchline for maximum impact.
119. **Use limiter release to avoid distortion** — Set master limiter release above 50 ms or use auto release to avoid low-frequency distortion from fast gain changes.
120. **Detect RMS for narration, peak for SFX** — Use RMS or program detection on narration compressors and peak detection on percussive SFX controls.
121. **Target −14 LUFS integrated** — Master the final export to around −14 LUFS integrated, which matches YouTube normalization and sits safely for TikTok.
122. **Don't chase louder than −12 LUFS** — Platforms turn loud uploads down; masters hotter than −12 LUFS gain nothing and lose transients.
123. **Consider −13 to −14 for TikTok** — TikTok often plays louder content less attenuated; aim −13 to −14 LUFS integrated and verify after upload.
124. **True peak ceiling** — Set the master limiter ceiling at −1 dBTP, or −1.5 dBTP if heavy transients remain, to survive AAC/Opus encoding.
125. **Measure true peak with oversampling** — Use a 4× or higher oversampled true-peak meter; sample-peak meters miss intersample overs.
126. **Measure dialogue loudness separately** — Measure narration-only loudness; it should sit roughly −16 to −14 LUFS with the full mix landing near −14.
127. **Narration-to-mix gap** — Keep narration within ~2 LU of the overall integrated loudness so it remains the dominant element.
128. **Short-term loudness ceiling** — Keep short-term loudness (3 s) below about −9 LUFS even at the loudest gag so normalization doesn't punish peaks.
129. **Momentary loudness spikes** — Watch momentary loudness (400 ms); spikes above −6 LUFS will feel jarring on earbuds even if integrated is correct.
130. **Loudness range target** — Aim for a loudness range (LRA) of 4–8 LU; wider loses quiet moments on phones, narrower sounds fatiguing.
131. **First 3 seconds at full loudness** — Make the opening 3 seconds sit at the target short-term loudness (around −14 to −12 LUFS) since scrolling viewers judge instantly.
132. **Don't start with silence** — Avoid more than 200 ms of silence at the head of the export; it reads as a broken video in the feed.
133. **Loop point awareness** — Shorts and TikTok loop, so ensure the final second and first second sit within ~3 LU of each other.
134. **Measure the whole program** — Measure integrated loudness over the full ~3 minute render, not a loud excerpt.
135. **Use gated loudness** — Follow ITU-R BS.1770 gated measurement; silences won't drag integrated loudness down, so don't over-compensate for pauses.
136. **Leave headroom for codecs** — Lossy encoding can add 0.5–1 dB of peak overshoot; −1 dBTP is a minimum, not a target to push into.
137. **Test the encoded file** — Re-measure loudness and true peak after encoding to AAC 128–320 kbps; adjust if peaks exceed −0.5 dBTP.
138. **Limit gently on the master** — Aim for no more than 2–4 dB of master limiter gain reduction at peaks; more means the mix needs rebalancing.
139. **Clip-to-limit chain** — Use a soft clipper shaving 1–2 dB before the limiter to reduce limiter workload and keep punch.
140. **Avoid loudness wars inside the mix** — Raise loudness by improving balance and density, not by pushing the limiter past 4 dB GR.
141. **Platform normalization doesn't boost much** — Quiet mixes below −16 LUFS may stay quiet on some platforms; never deliver below −16 LUFS integrated.
142. **Match loudness across episodes** — Keep each short within ±1 LU of −14 LUFS so a series plays consistently in a viewer's feed.
143. **Loudness meter on master always** — Keep an integrated/short-term/true-peak meter on the master bus visible throughout the mix, not just at export.
144. **Reference loudness-matched** — Compare against reference shorts only after matching them to the same LUFS, so you judge balance, not volume.
145. **Sample rate and bit depth** — Mix at 48 kHz/24-bit or 32-bit float to match video standards and preserve headroom.
146. **Dither on final reduction** — Apply dither only when reducing to 16-bit at the very last stage; skip it for 24-bit deliveries.
147. **Export at 48 kHz** — Deliver audio at 48 kHz to match video and avoid platform resampling artifacts.
148. **Avoid DC offset** — Check for and remove DC offset on all sources; it wastes headroom and can cause clicks at edits.
149. **Peak-normalize sources, not the master** — Peak-normalize individual recordings to around −6 dBFS for workflow consistency, but set master loudness with LUFS.
150. **Know your phone's playback loudness** — Remember many phones apply extra limiting at high volume; test on the device at max volume for distortion.
151. **Gain-stage at −18 dBFS RMS** — Set clip and track input levels so average signal sits near −18 dBFS RMS (0 VU) for plugins that model analog.
152. **Narration peaks around −10 dBFS** — Keep raw narration peaks around −10 to −6 dBFS before processing to leave room for EQ boosts.
153. **Mix bus headroom** — Keep the mix bus peaking around −6 dBFS before master processing so you have room for the limiter to work cleanly.
154. **Use clip gain before faders** — Fix level inconsistencies with clip gain first so faders stay near unity for fine automation.
155. **Keep faders near unity** — Aim for faders between −10 and +5 dB; extreme fader values signal a gain-staging error upstream.
156. **Watch plugin input levels** — Ensure each plugin's input isn't clipping internally, especially saturators and fixed-point emulations.
157. **Gain-match plugin bypass** — Set each plugin's output so bypassing it changes level by less than 0.5 dB.
158. **Avoid summing overload** — If more than 20 tracks feed a bus, lower each by 2–3 dB rather than adding a limiter on the bus.
159. **Bus structure by category** — Route tracks into Narration, Character Voices, SFX, Foley, Ambience and Music buses before a final Mix bus.
160. **Separate dialogue and effects stems** — Keep narration on its own path through to the master so a stem export for re-narration or translation is trivial.
161. **Narration bus first in priority** — Process the narration bus last in the decision chain; balance everything else against fixed narration.
162. **Effects bus subgroups** — Subdivide SFX into hard effects, comedic stingers and mimic calls so each category can be ducked or rebalanced independently.
163. **Music bus with dedicated ducker** — Insert the narration-keyed ducker on the music bus, not individual music tracks, for consistent behavior.
164. **Ambience bus low and wide** — Keep the ambience bus 15–25 dB below narration average and high-passed at 120–150 Hz.
165. **Reverb returns on aux buses** — Use shared reverb aux returns rather than inserts so space is consistent and adjustable from one fader.
166. **VCA for global balance** — Use VCAs to ride all non-narration elements together during dense narration passages.
167. **Pre-master bus** — Route all stems into a pre-master bus where tonal balance and glue compression happen, separate from the loudness limiter.
168. **Master chain order** — Order master processing as EQ, glue compression, saturation, soft clip, limiter, then true-peak meter.
169. **Keep master processing minimal** — Limit master EQ moves to ±2 dB; bigger fixes belong on the relevant bus.
170. **Check stem sum equals mix** — Null-test stems summed against the full mix; residual above −60 dBFS indicates routing or bus-processing errors.
171. **Name and color buses consistently** — Use fixed colors and names for every bus across episodes so mix templates transfer and errors are obvious.
172. **Build a mix template** — Save the bus structure, ducking routing, master chain and meters as a template so every short starts at the same calibrated state.
173. **Delay compensation on** — Ensure plugin delay compensation is active so sidechain ducking and parallel buses stay sample-aligned.
174. **Avoid phase issues in parallel paths** — Use the same latency on parallel compression paths or a null test will reveal comb filtering.
175. **Print processed narration** — Bounce the processed narration stem once settled so later bus tweaks don't change it unexpectedly.
176. **Master for mono first** — Check the final mix in mono; any element that drops more than 3 dB in mono needs fixing.
177. **Narration dead centre** — Pan narration exactly centre and keep it mono; phone speakers and single earbuds depend on it.
178. **Limit stereo width** — Keep wide elements' side content at least 6 dB below mid so mono collapse doesn't lose them.
179. **No hard-panned essentials** — Never hard-pan important SFX; a viewer with one earbud or a single phone speaker would miss it.
180. **Phone speaker check** — Play the export on at least two phone models' built-in speakers and confirm every narration word is intelligible.
181. **Earbud check** — Listen on common wireless earbuds at 50–60% volume and check that sub-bass isn't overwhelming and sibilance isn't harsh.
182. **Laptop speaker check** — Check on laptop speakers, which roll off below ~150 Hz like phones but add midrange resonance.
183. **Car or room check** — Listen from across a room on a phone; if narration is still clear, the mix will survive noisy environments.
184. **Noisy-environment test** — Play the mix over 60 dB SPL background noise (café recording); narration must remain intelligible.
185. **Low-volume intelligibility check** — Play at ~20% phone volume; if any line is lost, raise narration or cut competing elements in 1–4 kHz.
186. **Small-speaker simulation** — Mix part-time through a band-pass emulation (300 Hz–8 kHz) or a single small monitor to catch phone translation issues.
187. **Studio monitors for detail** — Use full-range monitors or headphones to clean low-end and noise problems phones can't reveal.
188. **Calibrate monitoring** — Calibrate monitors to a consistent level (e.g., 79 dB SPL at −20 dBFS pink noise per speaker) to make loudness decisions repeatable.
189. **Check after upload** — Download or screen-record the uploaded short on each platform to confirm loudness, codec artifacts and sync.
190. **Sync check after encode** — Verify audio-video sync within ±1 frame (about ±42 ms at 24 fps) after export; early audio of 20+ ms on lip flaps is noticeable.
191. **Check for codec artifacts** — Listen for swirly or watery artifacts on reverb tails and noise; reduce dense high-frequency content if they appear.
192. **Distortion check at max volume** — Play at full phone volume; rattling or crackling means too much 100–300 Hz energy for the speaker.
193. **Silent-autoplay awareness** — Many viewers start muted, so ensure the first unmuted second lands on narration or a clear sound, not a fade-in.
194. **Fresh-ears pass** — Take at least a 10-minute break before final balance decisions; ear fatigue skews high-frequency judgment.
195. **Mix at moderate level** — Mix mostly at 70–80 dB SPL and briefly check loud and quiet; prolonged loud mixing distorts perception and harms hearing.
196. **Reference against successful shorts** — Compare to two or three well-performing narrated animation shorts loudness-matched to −14 LUFS for balance.
197. **Spectral comparison against reference** — Overlay your mix's long-term spectrum on a reference's; differences over 3 dB in any octave warrant attention.
198. **Check transitions for level jumps** — Scrub every scene cut for sudden loudness jumps over 3 LU that aren't intentional comedic choices.
199. **Final loudness log** — Record integrated LUFS, LRA, true peak and narration loudness for each export to maintain consistency across the series.
200. **Last check is narration only** — Before delivery, listen once focusing solely on narration; if any word needs effort to understand, fix it before publishing.
