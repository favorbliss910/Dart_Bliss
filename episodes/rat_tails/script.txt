In 1902,{nineteen oh two} a rat catcher in Hanoi cut the tail off a rat and let it go. Then he got paid for it. It all started a few years earlier, when the French rulers of Hanoi built something they were very proud of. A brand new sewer, miles of clean stone tunnels under the fancy French part of town. Turns out rats loved it too. It was dark, it was warm, and every pipe led straight to a kitchen. Basically a five-star{five star} hotel with free room service. And rats carry fleas and fleas carry plague. So the city makes an offer. Bring us a dead rat and we'll pay you one cent. At first people haul them in by the bucket. Then by the cart load. Soon the health office is buried in dead rats and it smells exactly like you'd think. So the officials change one tiny rule. Don't bring the whole rat, just bring the tail. And that's when our rat catcher does what any sane person would do. He climbs down into the sewer, grabs the fattest rat he can find, snips off its tail, and gently sets it free. The other catchers think he's lost it. One of them goes, you're supposed to kill it, not give it a haircut. He just smiles and pockets the tail. The first week he turns in a hundred tails. By the end of the month, a thousand. The city is thrilled. On one day in June, the people of Hanoi bring in over 20,000{twenty thousand} tails. The officials do the math and decide the rats will be gone by Christmas. Now here's the weird part. Our rat catcher starts buying rice. Sacks of it. More rice than his whole family could eat in a year. He carries it out of town past the rice fields to a little wooden shed. Wait what? Is he feeding them? Word gets around. The rat man has gone soft. The other catchers start calling his shed the rat hotel. A French official stops him in the street and goes, the plague is the enemy, monsieur. Not your little friends. Meanwhile, the tails keep pouring in. 30,000,{thirty thousand} 40,000,{forty thousand} and somehow the sewers are just as full as ever. Then one night, a health inspector is walking home through the French Quarter. Something runs over his shoe. A rat, fat, healthy, and no tail. Then another, then ten more. The inspectors follow the trail out of the city, past the rice fields to a little wooden shed. They open the door, cages, hundreds of them, stacked to the ceiling, every rat chewing on rice. You see, the city never paid for dead rats. It paid for tails and a rat without a tail is still a rat. It can still have dozens of babies a year and every one of those babies grows a brand new tail. So every rat he set free wasn't a mistake. It was an investment. It's like paying a gardener for every weed he pulls. A smart gardener leaves the roots. In real life, the bounty backfired so badly that Hanoi shut it down. He never wanted to get rid of the rats. In Hanoi, the tail was the money and the rat was the bank.
